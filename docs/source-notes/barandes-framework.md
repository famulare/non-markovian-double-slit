# Source notes: Barandes's stochastic framework

These are locator notes and paraphrases, not a substitute for the papers.

## The Stochastic-Quantum Theorem, arXiv:2309.03085v2

- **Pages 8-12, Eqs. 24-42:** exact definition of the tuple
  \((\mathcal C,\mathcal T,\mathcal T_0,\Gamma,p,\mathcal A)\). Important
  semantic detail: \(\mathcal A\) is the maximal algebra of random variables
  \(A:\mathcal C\times\mathcal T\to\mathbb R\), not an event algebra.
- **Pages 9-11, Eqs. 25-39:** \(\Gamma\) is defined only for conditioning
  times in the sparse set \(\mathcal T_0\), is column-stochastic, trivializes at
  conditioning times, and composes between division events. A generic target
  time need not supply another conditioning law.
- **Pages 11-12:** higher-order conditional probabilities are not fixed. The
  tuple represents an equivalence class of non-Markovian realizers.
- **Pages 17-19, Eq. 69:** every finite indivisible stochastic process can be
  regarded as a subsystem of a unistochastic process.
- **Pages 26-28, Eqs. 105-110:** explicit dilation and conclusion; the dilated
  Hilbert dimension is at most \(N^3\).

Source: [arXiv:2309.03085](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.03085).

## The Stochastic-Quantum Correspondence, arXiv:2302.10778v3

- **Pages 3-5, Eqs. 1-8:** transition probabilities and indivisibility.
- **Pages 5-6, Eqs. 9-11:** the tower of higher-order conditionals and the
  decision to leave whole-trajectory probabilities unspecified.
- **Pages 6-11, Eqs. 12-31:** stochastic-quantum dictionary, Kraus form,
  dilation, and \(\Gamma_{ij}=|U_{ij}|^2\).
- **Pages 12-14, Eqs. 33-43:** Hamiltonian/Schrodinger machinery and the
  interference residual generated when entrywise squared moduli fail to
  compose.
- **Page 8, around Eqs. 20-23:** for a pure initial configuration, the state
  vector is one column of the law-like time-evolution matrix; it is not an
  additional ontic variable.

Source: [arXiv:2302.10778](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.10778).

## Quantum Systems as Indivisible Stochastic Processes, arXiv:2507.21192v1

This later synthesis develops the interpretation, measurement framework, and
the role of Hilbert-space ingredients as representational tools. It supports the
project's use of standard Hamiltonian methods to construct highly general
stochastic laws. It does not require an autonomous derivation of the free
Hamiltonian from positive probabilities.

Source: [arXiv:2507.21192](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.21192).
